1. Range - Region between the limits within which a quantity is measured
2. Span - The algebraic difference between the upper and lower range values.
3. Elevated Zero Range - A range in which the zero value of the measured variable, measured signal, is greater than the lower range value. (-25 to 50 psi)
4. Suppressed Zero Range - A range in which the zero value of the measured variable is less than the lower range value. (20 to 100 psi)
5. Measured Variable - Temperature, Pressure, etc.
6. Measured Signal - The electrical, mechanical, pneumatic or other variable applied to the input of a device.
7. Output Signal - A signal delivered by a device, element or system.
8. Accuracy - deviation from true value
	1. Percentage of full scale reading
	2. Percentage of span
	3. Measured Variable Accuracy of ± 1 Kpa,
	4. Percentage of the actual reading. Thus, for a ± 2% of reading voltmeter, we would have an inaccuracy of ± 0.04 volts for a reading of 2 volts.
9. Precision - difference between the instruments reported values during repeated measurements of the same quantity
10. Repeatability - ability of an instrument to reproduce the same measurement each time the same set of conditions is repeated
11. Sensitivity
12. Resolution - The smallest increment of change in the measured valve that can be determined from the instrument readout scale
13. Dead band - the range through which an input signal may be varied upon reversal of direction, without initiating an observable change in output signal
14. Hysteresis - An instrument is said to exhibit hysteresis when there is a difference in readings depending on whether the value of the measured quantity is approached from above or below.
	- Hysteresis results from the inelastic quantity of an element or device.
	- it may be the result of mechanical friction, magnetic effects, elastic deformation, or thermal effects
	- Hysteresis is expressed in percent of span. Dead band term is included in the hysteresis.
