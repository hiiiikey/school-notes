Failure is an occurs when material starts exhibiting inelastic behavior, or when the element separates into two or more pieces, has become distorted, etc.

# Types of Failure
1. Yielding:
   - Occurs in ductile materials
   - Due to excessive inelastic deformation
     
2. Fracture:
   - Occurs in brittle materials
   - The component tears apart in two or more parts

N.B. - A rough guideline is that if percentage elongation is less than 5% then the material may be treated as brittle and if it is more than 5% then the material is ductile

## Special Instances
There are many instances when a ductile material may fail by fracture, such as:
1. Cyclic loading
2. Long term static loading at higher temperatures
3. Impact loading
4. Work hardening
5. Severe quenching


| Ductile Fracture               | Brittle Fracture                       |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Slow, stable crack propagation | Very quick, unsteady crack propagation |
| Slant face fracture            | Flat face fracture                     |
| Necking occurs                 | No necking                             |
| Cup and cone fractures         | Chevron marks and radial lines         |
# Static and Variable Loading

## 1. Static loading
- Stationary force, or couple, applied to a member
- Unchanging in magnitude

## 2. Variable loading
- Load may change in magnitude with respect to time
	- Weight of traffic passing over a bridge
