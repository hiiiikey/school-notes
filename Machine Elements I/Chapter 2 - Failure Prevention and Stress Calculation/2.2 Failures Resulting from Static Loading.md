# I. Ductile Materials
Tensile and Compressive Strengths are equal

## 1. Maximum-Normal (Principal) Stress Theory

**MAIN PRINCIPLE:** Yielding begins when the maximum principal stress in a stress element exceeds the yield strength

For any stress element, use Mohr's circle to find the principal stresses. And then, compare the largest principal stress to the yield strength.

This theory is not safe to use for ductile materials.

## 2. Maximum Shear Stress Theory

**MAIN PRINCIPLE:** Yielding begins when the maximum shear stress in a stress element exceeds the maximum shear stress $\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}$

- For a tension test specimen, maximum shear stress is $\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}$.
- At yielding stress point, $\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}=\frac{\text{Yield Stress}(\sigma_{y})}{2}$

For any stress element, use Mohr’s circle to find the maximum shear stress. Compare the maximum shear stress to Sy/2.

- Order the principal stresses such that $\sigma_{1} ≥ \sigma_{2} ≥ \sigma_{3}$
- $\tau_{max}=\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{3}}{2} \geq \frac{\sigma_{y}}{2}$ or $\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{3} \geq \sigma_{y}$
-  $\tau_{max}=\frac{\sigma_{y}}{2n} \text{ where n is the factor of safety}$

# 3. Distortion Energy Failure Theory
**MAIN PRINCIPLE:** Yielding occurs when the distortion strain energy per unit volume reaches the distortion strain energy per unit volume for yield in simple tension or compression of the same material. 

Hydrostatic stress is average of middle stress, ($\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}$)

$$
U_{d}=\frac{1+v}{3E}S^2_{y}
$$

DE theory predicts failure when distortion energy, exceeds distortion energy of tension test specimen

$$
\sigma' = \sqrt{ \frac{(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})^2 + (\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3})^2 + (\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{1})}{2}} \geq S_{y}
$$
Middle side is defined as von Mises Stress.

From Plane Stress, simplifies to:
$$\sigma'=\sqrt{(\sigma_{A}^2-\sigma_{A}\sigma_{B}+\sigma_{B}^2)}$$
$$\text{Factor of Safety}=\frac{\sigma_{y}}{\sigma'}$$
For pure shear loading, Mohr's circle shows that $\sigma_{A}=\sigma_{B}=\tau$

# II. Brittle Materials
Compressive strengths are usually larger than tensile strengths

## 1. Maximum Normal Stress Theory
- Not recommended for use
- Included for historical comparison
  
**MAIN PRINCIPLE:** Failure occurs when the maximum principal stress in a stress element exceeds the strength. 

Predicts failure when $\sigma_{1}\geq S_{ut}$ or $\sigma_{3} \leq -S_{uc}$

$$
\text{Factor of Safety} = \frac{S_{ut}}{\sigma_{A}}=\frac{-S_{uc}}{\sigma_{B}}
$$

# Selection of Failure Criteria
For Ductile:
- MSS is conservative, often used for design where higher reliability is desired
- DE is typical, often used for analysis where agreement with experimental data is desired
- If tensile and compressive strengths differ, use Ductile Coulomb-Mohr

For Brittle:
- Mohr theory is best, but difficult to use
- Brittle Coulomb-Mohr is very conservative in 4th quadrant
- Modified Mohr is still slightly conservative in 4th quadrant, but closer to typical
