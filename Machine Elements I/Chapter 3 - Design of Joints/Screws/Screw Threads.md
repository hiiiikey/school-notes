There are two distinct uses for screw threads and theybusually demand different behavior from the threads:
a. Power Screw
b. Threaded Fasteners

# 1. Power Screws
![[Pasted image 20250526000601.png|300]]

Imagine that a single thread of the screw is unrolled or developed (Fig. 3-6) for exactly a single turn. 
- On edge of the thread will form the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose base is the circumference of the mean-thread- circle and whose height is the lead. 
- The angle λ is the lead angle of the thread.
- For raising the load a force $P_{R}$ acts to the right and to lower the load, $P_{L}$ acts to the left

![[Pasted image 20250526000746.png|400]]

Do some statics, and you get:
### Force
$$
P_{Raise}\text{(or Force Raise)}=\frac{\pi fd_{m}+l}{\pi d_{m}-fl} , \text{ where f is friction coefficient}
$$
$$
P_{Lower}\text{(or Force Lower)}=\frac{\pi fd_{m}-l}{\pi d_{m}+fl} , \text{ where f is friction coefficient}
$$
### Torque
$$T=F \text{ x } R, \text{ where R = mean radius}=\frac{d_m}{2}$$
$$
T_{Raise}\text{(or Torque Raise)}=\left( \frac{F.dm}{2} \right)\frac{\pi fd_{m}\sec \alpha+l}{\pi d_{m}-fl\sec \alpha} , \text{ where f is friction coefficient}
$$
$$
T_{Lower}\text{(or Torque Lower)}=\left( \frac{F.dm}{2} \right) \frac{\pi fd_{m}\sec \alpha-l}{\pi d_{m}+fl\sec \alpha} , \text{ where } \alpha=\text{angle of thread contact}, 
$$
IF $\pi fd_{m}>l, T_{L}>0$, also known as "self locking"
IF $T_{L}<0$, no torque is needed to lower the load

### Efficiency

$$
\epsilon=\frac{T_{o}}{T_{R}}, \text{where T(o) is T at f=0 (no friction)}
$$
$$
\epsilon=\frac{Fl}{2\pi T_{R}}
$$
## Power Screws with Collar

$$
T_{c}=\frac{Ff_{c}d_{c}}{2}
\text{ then, add this to } T_{R} \text{ or } T_{L}
