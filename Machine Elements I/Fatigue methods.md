# The Stress-Life Method
- Least accurate, particularly for low cycle applications-
- Most traditional, easiest to implement
- Test specimens are subjected to repeated stress while counting cycles to failure



# The Strain-Life Method
## Relation of Fatigue Life to Strain
$$
\frac{\Delta \epsilon}{2}=\frac{\sigma'_{F}}{E}(2N)^{b}+\epsilon'_{F}(2N)^{c}

$$

where
-  $\epsilon'_{f}=\text{true strain corresponding to fracture in one reversal}$
- $\sigma'_{f}=\text{true stress corresponding to fracture in one reversal}$
- $\text{fatigue ductility exponent c} = \text{slope of plastic-strain line}$
- $\text{fatigue strength exponent b} = \text{slope of elastic-strain line}$
- $N=\text{Number of Cycles before Fatigue Fracture}$
- 

Known as Manson-Coffin Relationship between fatigue life and total strain
Equation has limited use for design since values for total strain at
discontinuities are not readily available

# The Endurance Limit
- The endurance limit for steels has been experimentally found to be related to the ultimate strength
- Simplified estimate of endurance limit for steels for the rotating-beam specimen:

$$
S'_{e}=0.5S_{ult.tensile} \text{ if } S_{ult.tensile}\leq 200kpsi (1400 MPa)
$$
$$
S'_{e}=100kpsi(700MPa) \text{ if } S_{ult.tensile} > 200 kpsi (1400 MPa)
$$


# Fatigue Strength
$$
S'_{F}(N) =  \frac{E\Delta \epsilon_{e}}{2}=\sigma'_{F}(2N)^b = fS_{ut}
$$
Substitute number of cycles for N

f=fraction of $S_{ut}$ represented by $S'_{f}$
