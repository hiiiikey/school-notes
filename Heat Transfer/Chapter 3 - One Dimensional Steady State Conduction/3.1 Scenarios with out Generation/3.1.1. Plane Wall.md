# Heat Flux
## Temperature Distribution Without Generation

For steady-state conditions with no distributed source or sink of energy within the wall, the appropriate form of the heat equation is:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(k \frac{dT}{dx})=0
$$

![[Pasted image 20250412190632.png|300]]

where:
- $T_{\infty,1}$ = Outside temperature on Side 1
- $T_{\infty,2}$ = Outside ambient temperature on Side 2
- $T_{s,1}$ = Surface Temperature of wall on Side 1
- $T_{s,2}$ = Surface Temperature of wall on Side 2


Hence, it follows that, for one-dimensional, steady-state conduction in a plane wall with ***no heat generation***, the heat flux is a constant, independent of x.

Therefore, assuming k is constant:

$$
k\left( \frac{d^2T}{dx^2} \right)=0
$$

$$
T(x)=C_{1}x + C_{2}
$$

### Utilize Boundary conditions

At x=0,
$T(0)=T_{s,1}$ 

And at x=L,
$T(L)=T_{s,2}$

Therefore: $$
T(x)=(T_{s,2}-T_{s,1})(\frac{x}{L})+T_{s,1}
$$
- For one-dimensional, steady-state conduction in a plane wall with no heat generation and constant thermal conductivity, the temperature varies linearly with x.

Apply Fourier's Law:
$$
q''(x)=\frac{q(x)}{A}=\frac{-k}{L}(T_{s,2}-T_{s,1})
$$
# Thermal Resistance

For **Conduction**
$$
\Large{R_{thermal,conduction} = \frac{(T_{s,1}-{T_{s,2}})}{q_{x}}=\frac{L}{kA}}
$$
For **Convection**
$$
R_{thermal,covecntion} = \frac{(T_{s}-T_{\infty})}{q_{x}}
$$

but

$$
\dot{q}=hA(T_{s}-T_{\infty})
$$
therefore:

$$
\Large{R_{thermal,covecntion}=\frac{(T_{s}-T_{\infty})}{q_{x}}=\frac{1}{hA}}
$$

Hence:

$$
R_{tot}=\frac{1}{h_{1}A}+\frac{L}{kA}+\frac{1}{h_{2}A}
$$
