# 3. Permanent Mold Casting Processes
A permanent mold, called the die may be made from which a large number of castings can be produced, the molds are usually made of cast iron or steel, although graphite, copper and aluminum have been used as mold materials.
### Advantages
- Dense casting with superior mechanical properties
- Castings are quite uniform in shape
- Consistent quality of finish on castings

### Disadvantages 
- Cost of tooling is high
- Process is generally limited to production of small castings of simple exterior design

### Applications
- suitable for small and medium sized casting such as carburetor bodies, oil pump bodies, connecting rods, pistons etc.
- widely suitable for non-ferrous castings

# 4. Special Casting Processes
## 4.1 Centrifugal Casting Process
- molten metal is poured into a revolving mold and allowed to solidify molten metal by pressure of centrifugal force
- Castings by this process are free from impurities
- castings produced will be of high density type and of good strength.

### Advantages
- Easy formation of hollow cylinders
- Less material required for gate
- Fine grained structure at the outer surface of casting
- Free of shrinkage cavities and porosity

### Disadvantages
- Contamination of surfaces with non-metallic inclusions
- More segregation of alloy component during pouring under the forces of rotation

### Applications
Casting cylindrical parts and pipes

## 4.2 Investment Casting Process
- aka Lost Wax Process
- patterns are prepared by injecting wax or polystyrene in a metal dies
- ![[Pasted image 20250428022539.png|300]]![[Pasted image 20250428022555.png|300]]

### Advantages
- Wax can carry very fine details – so the process not only gives good dimensional tolerances, but also excellent surface finish
### Disadvantages
- Costly
- Long production time
- Size limitations
- Complex process control

## 4.3 Vacuum Casting Process
- aka counter-gravity casting
- same process as investment casting, except for the step of filling the mold
- the material is sucked upwards into the mould by a vacuum pump
- One advantage of vacuum casting is that by releasing the pressure a short time after the mold is filled, we can release the un-solidified metal back into the flask. This allows us to create hollow castings
- portion of the metal closest to the mold surface always solidifies first
