# Iron
Most common casting material is **Iron** 
1. Gray cast iron:
	- 2.5–4% carbon, 1–3% silicon, and 0.4–1% manganese
	- Cheap, and most commonly used material
	- Good for intricate features
	- Low shrinkage rate, easy to machine
	- Typically used in manufacturing of engine blocks
2. White cast iron:
	- Has lower silicon content, and lower melting point
	- Hard, but brittle
	- Abrasive-resistant
	- grinding mills, wear surfaces of pumps, balls and rings of coal pulverisers, etc.
3. Malleable iron
	- 2–3.3% carbon, 0.6–1.2% silicon, and 0.25–0.65% manganese
	- obtained by heat-treating white iron castings
	- High strength, and high ductility
	- must be hardened in order to increase its relatively low wear resistance
	- camshaft brackets, differential carriers, and numerous housings
4. Ductile cast iron
	- 3–4% carbon, 1.8–2.8% silicon, and 0.15–0.9% manganese
	- can also be cast into thin sections (though not as well as gray cast iron)
	- superior in machinability to gray cast iron
	- corrosion and wear resistance is superior to steel and equivalent to gray cast iron
	- applications include gears, crankshafts, and cams.

# Other Casting Materials

## Aluminum and Magnesium Alloys

1. Aluminum:
	- Difficult material to cast, needs to be alloyed
	- Provides good fluidity, has low shrinkage, and good resistance to cracking
	- Mechanical properties obtainable for aluminum alloys depend on the content of the alloying elements as well as on heat-treatment processes
2. Magnesium:
	- Difficult material to cast in its pure form
	- Alloys of it have excellent corrosion resistance and moderate strengths
3. Copper:
	1. Bronze (80–90% copper, 5–20% tin, and less then 1–2% of lead, zinc, phosphorous, nickel, and iron)
4. Steel castings:
	1. isotropic uniformity of properties, compared to cast iron
	2. strength and ductility of steel becomes a problem
	3. high shrinkage rates
	4. Low carbon steels are found in automotive apps, and high carbon steels are used for tools and die making
