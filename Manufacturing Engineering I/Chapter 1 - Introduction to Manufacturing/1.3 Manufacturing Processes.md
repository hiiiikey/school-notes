# Manufacturing Processes

## A. Shaping Processes
- Alter the geometry of the starting work material.
### 1. Solidification Processes
- Starting material is a heated liquid or semifluid form.
- ![[Pasted image 20250426232508.png|500]]
#### Steps for casting process
1. Mold is created
2. Material is heated to a specified temperature
3. Molten material is poured to mold cavity
4. Molten material solidifies
5. Mold is removed
6. Casting is cleaned, finished, and inspected
### 2. Particulate Processes
- starting material consists of powders.
- ![[Pasted image 20250426233037.png|500]]

### 3. Deformation Processes (Forming)
- Starting material is a ductile solid.
- Change the shape of a given raw stock without changing the its phase (without melting)
- Involves **beating with a hammer**, squeezing, bending, etc.
- Examples: Aluminum/steel frame of doors, windows, coins, springs, cables, wires, sheet metal etc.
- Includes the following processes:
	1. Rolling
		- Used 90% of times
		- The compressive forces applied by the rolls reduce the thickness of the material or changes its cross sectional area.
		- Hot rolls are generally rough so that they can bite the work, and cold rolls are ground and polished for good finish.
		- ![[Pasted image 20250426233716.png|350]]
		- ![[Pasted image 20250426234123.png|300]]
		
	2. Forging
		- Oldest method of manufacturing
		- Involved plastic deformation of material between two dies to achieve desired configuration/shape
		- **Open-Die Forging**: metal is compressed by repeated blows by a hammer and is manipulated manually
			- ![[Pasted image 20250426234510.png|250]]
		- **Closed-Die Forging**: desired configuration is achieved by squeezing the workpiece between two shaped and closed dies
			- ![[Pasted image 20250426234521.png|300]]
		  
	3. Extrusion
		- Relatively new process
		- material is compressed in a chamber and the deformed material is forced to flow through the die
		- die opening corresponds to the cross section of the required product
		- basically a hot working process, however, for softer materials cold extrusion is also performed
		- Direct and Indirect extrusion purposes exist
			- ![[Pasted image 20250426234648.png|300]]
	4. Drawing (Tube, and Wire)
		- The material is pulled through a die in order to reduce it to the desired shape and size
	5. Deep drawing
		- extensively used to for making cylindrical shaped parts such as cups, shells, etc from sheet metal
		- As the blank is drawn into the die cavity by compressive stress applied by the punch force  and finally retracts.
		- The blank is a piece of sheet metal which is cut to the required size and the die has a cavity in the shape required
		- EThe punch is of the same  shape to the die cavity and the difference b/n the punch and the die cavity is just sufficient to allow the sheet to be pushed by the punch into the die cavity.
		- ![[Pasted image 20250426234916.png|400]]
### 4. Material removal process 
- Starting material is a ductile or brittle solid.
- Excess material is removed from the starting piece so what remains is the desired geometry
- Examples:
	1. Conventional Machining
		- Drilling, milling, etc.
	2. Abrasive Processes
		- Grinding
	3. Nontraditional Processes
		- Sharp cutting tools
